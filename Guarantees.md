# Guarantees

What AGBoss promises when several instances move the same execution trees and their steps write to
databases and call other systems — each guarantee, the exact assumptions it rests on, what breaks
when an assumption does not hold, and the test or checker that proves it (#533). The mechanisms
are on [Several-instances](Several-instances), the store contracts on
[Your-own-store](Your-own-store), the provider's side on [Payment-gateways](Payment-gateways), the payment races on
[Payment-scenarios](Payment-scenarios), the fault scenarios on [Guarantees-across-processes](Guarantees-across-processes), and what the
platform must provide for them to hold (grace, probes, clocks, one database) on
[Deploying-on-Kubernetes](Deploying-on-Kubernetes).

## The guarantees

| # | Guarantee | Said exactly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P1** | One owner per tree | At any moment at most one instance trusts a tree's lease; every epoch is granted once; no write is accepted under a superseded, released, finished or expired token. |
| **P2** | One effect per business key | A fenced effect runs its write once per step, or once per tenant, name and business key; a repeat replays the stored result while it is kept (A12); another fingerprint is `BusinessKeyConflictError`. |
| **P3** | One send per attempt | An outbound call sends an attempt only after it claimed it (a compare-and-set) and only while its lease holds; a pending attempt whose owner lives is never resent; an attempt is sent again only after a definite failure or a `notSent()` of `reconcile` — an error of unknown outcome is reconciled first (#657), and a `KeyReplay`'s `notSent()` past its `keyRetention` is never sent (#658, #795); the other system is charged once per business key. |
| **P4** | No takeover of finished work | Once a lease is finished, no `acquire` of its key gets it again: no instance takes finished work over, and the audit log has no Takeover row for it. |
| **P5** | Compensation only on real failure, once | An undo runs only when a step really failed or was cancelled — never because its lease was handed over; once it is marked done it never runs again, on any instance (#607); between its effect and its mark it may run again under its own key, so it is exactly once when that effect is fenced. |
| **P6** | One execution per A2A task (#807) | An A2A task's id is its root execution's reserved id, kept by the lease store when the execution starts (the claim every execution id goes through): a resend, a second instance or a retry after a crash never starts a second execution; a sweep that fences an id no execution started under makes it unusable for good. A cancel of a task stops that execution only — here, on another instance, at rest or before its record exists — never another run of its context. A task's status is final only once its execution's records show the end; a task never carries an error's own text (its code and that code's fixed text only). |
| **P7** | No effect after a durable cancel (#909) | Once `app.cancel` has resolved, no fenced effect runs its write and no outbound call is recorded or sent for the tree, except its own undo; a result stored before replays; a write already in its transaction may finish first; a send already claimed may still be answered and recorded. On every instance, with no window: the fencing stores read the cancel's mark in the lease's own transaction, so a holder that has not yet seen the request at a renewal (up to 10 s) is refused at its next fenced write all the same — the step stops, the tree unwinds as cancelled, and its undo runs. |
| **P8** | No call waits forever on a store (#928) | No call waits forever on a store: under a lease a call is bounded by the time the holder trusts the lease and, unanswered, hands the tree over with its outcome unknown — reconciled by the next holder, never charged twice; any other call is bounded by 15 s and fails as a store error (retention deletes and A2A file bodies: 5 min). A timed-out call is never taken as done or as not done: the next holder replays the fenced effect's row (P2), reconciles a pending intent with the other system (P3), resumes from the latest checkpoint, or reads the lost answer again (#720). A lease renewal that does not answer is not a hand-over: it is bounded at 5 s and the renewals run on a fixed cadence of a quarter of the lease (7.5 s), so of the three tries inside the 29 s the holder trusts, any two may fail — hung or fast — and the third confirm; three in a row cost the lease, and the holder stops at its fence. |

## The assumptions

Each guarantee holds while every assumption it lists holds.

| # | Assumption | Needed by | When it does not hold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1** | **Store conformance.** The stores pass `storeConformance` — every contract, the store set's cases and the interleaving explorer — and `faultSuite` against a real database. | P1–P6 | Whatever rule the store breaks: a second live holder, a stale write accepted, a double charge, finished work taken — and an undo on a hand-over (a lease store that grants one epoch twice makes scenario 5c undo a reservation that did not fail). A store only as good as the cases: a business-key column with an accent-insensitive collation passed them all until #620. |
| **A2** | **One transactional backend.** The fenced-effect and outbound-call stores declare the lease store's `backend` and check the lease in the write's own transaction (`LeaseCheck.Atomic`). | P1–P3 | A holder whose lease was just taken over can still write or record: a double charge or a second owner's write. The opt-out `LeaseCheck.WithoutDoubleChargeProtection` says so, and assembly warns. |
| **A3** | **The database's clock is slewed, never stepped** (NTP slew only), and it is one clock: a failover to a primary or replica whose clock differs is a step. On PostgreSQL it is the primary server's `clock_timestamp()`, read inside each lease statement ([Running-on-PostgreSQL](Running-on-PostgreSQL#what-the-guarantees-assume-of-postgresql)). | P1, P3 | A forward step expires live leases early: the store grants the tree to another instance while the holder still trusts its lease, for up to the step's size beyond the margin. Its fenced writes are refused (A2), but P1's "at most one instance trusts the lease" does not hold, and a send it already decided rests on the provider's G1 (A7). A backward step keeps a dead holder's lease alive longer: liveness only. No test steps the database's clock; the core's tests step the holder's wall clock. |
| **A4** | **Drift bound:** ρ_holder + ρ_store ≤ 1 s / 30 s (the safety margin over one lease). | P1, P3 | A holder whose clock runs slower than the bound trusts its lease past the store's expiry: another instance can take it while it still sends. |
| **A5** | **No pause that stops both of the holder's clocks while the database's time runs on.** The fence ends at whichever count runs out first, monotonic or wall: a host suspend stops `performance.now()`, but the wall clock ends the fence early. | P1, P3 | A pause that freezes both (some VM pauses) leaves the holder trusting an expired lease when it wakes: its next send can coincide with the new holder's. Its fenced writes are still refused (A2). |
| **A6** | **`attemptTimeout` + lease bound.** A send in flight keeps its lease renewed, bounded by `outboundCalls.attemptTimeout` (default 2 min) and the instance's stop; past it the holder stops renewing, so the call is in flight for at most `attemptTimeout` plus up to the lease's 30 s. | P3 | A send still hung past the bound can land after the next owner reconciled it and sent again: two charges unless the provider deduplicates (A7). |
| **A7** | **The provider's G1–G6** ([Payment-gateways](Payment-gateways)): it deduplicates on `call.idempotencyKey` (G1), can look a call up (G2), keeps keys long enough (G3), answers a repeat with the first response (G4), refuses another request under one key (G5), and tells late outcomes (G6). | P3 | Without G1 a send that A6 does not bound is a second charge. Without G2 or G6 a pending intent cannot be reconciled and needs manual review (`OutboundCallUnreconciledError`). |
| **A8** | **A shared idempotency key** (`@Application({ idempotencyKeyField })`, #825) across the instances (business-key fingerprints), and a shared `piiKeyField` (business-key handles). | P2, P3 | Assembly refuses business keys without it (`[application.idempotency-key-required]`); never an unkeyed digest. A rotation names the old key as `previousIdempotencyKeyField` until no intent recorded under it can be retried: then compared under both, stored under the new one; removed, what is left of it is a conflict (fail closed), never a second call. |
| **A9** | **Writes and sends happen inside a step's frame**, under its lease. | P1–P3 | Frames without a lease (a reply interpreter, `maxSpend()`, an eval judge, an MCP-exported tool) have no fence: what they write or send is unfenced. |
| **A10** | **Rate-limit and tree slots are not money.** They are counted on the caller's clock. | — | A skewed instance may overshoot a rate limit or a tree's concurrency cap. It never double-charges: P1–P3 do not depend on them. |
| **A11** | **The other system is the only side effect.** Business rows live in the store's own database, written in the effect's transaction. | P2 | A row written elsewhere (another database, a file) is outside the transaction: it can be written twice or kept after a rollback. |
| **A12** | **A repeat comes while its row is kept.** A fenced effect's result is kept for `retention.fencedEffects` (default 24 h), an ended outbound call's intent for `retention.outboundCalls` (default 168 h); a pending intent is never removed. | P2, P3 | A repeat under the same key after its row was removed runs its write, or sends, again: a second charge unless the gateway still keeps the key (G3) or the application's own row refuses it ([Payment scenarios](Payment-scenarios#ps17-the-idempotency-key-expires-before-a-retry)). |
| **A13** | **None on the host's wall clock for a thread's latest checkpoint.** LangGraph names each checkpoint by a UUIDv6 of `Date.now()` and a saver's latest checkpoint is the greatest id, so after a backward step of the host's clock (an NTP `settimeofday`) a new checkpoint sorts before older ones. | — (results, not the P list) | Nothing reads the greatest id as the latest: a child's end is read at the checkpoint its own run wrote last (a re-entry and a remote child's parent at its thread's leaf, #661), and every other read — a resume, a recovery, a re-entered child, an undo's entries — goes through the application's checkpointer, which marks a thread once an id sorts below its greatest and reads a marked thread by LangGraph's step (#682, on any saver: in-memory, SQLite, PostgreSQL). `tests/flow/runtime/child-clock-step.test.ts`, `tests/app/clock-step-resume.test.ts`, `tests/app/ordered-checkpoint-saver.test.ts`. A checkpointer read through `app.resolve(BaseCheckpointSaver)` is the listed saver itself, unordered. |
| **A14** | **MongoDB** ([MongoDB](MongoDB)): a replica set (or a sharded cluster); every write acknowledged by a majority on the journal (`w=majority`, `journal=true`), reads at `majority` from the primary, transactions at `snapshot`, retryable writes on, the stores' collections in the simple collation — each refused at start when not so; and what no setting enforces: the members' clocks synchronized (a lease's expiry is the primary's `$$NOW`, a failover hands it to another member's clock — A3), a fenced effect's work within `transactionLifetimeLimitSeconds`. | P1–P3 | A write acknowledged by fewer than a majority is rolled back by a failover: the lease is gone and another instance takes the same epoch — two owners (`tests/integration/failover.test.ts` shows it at `w=1`). A case-insensitive collation makes two business keys one: a replay of another order's charge. A member's clock ahead of the others expires live leases early once it is the primary. |
| **A15** | **Only a definite failure is marked definite** (#657). `isDefiniteFailure(error)` returns `true` only for an error that proves the other system did not carry the call out (its validation refused it, a decline); anything it does not recognise is left of unknown outcome, the default. | P3 | An error marked definite after the other system acted (a 5xx after the charge) is resent with no lookup: one charge while the gateway keeps the key (G1), a second one after it. |
| **A16** | **The key's retention is declared where a replay stands in for a lookup** (#658). Every call declares how far its `notSent()` is trusted (#795, `notSent`, required): one that relies on the other system replaying the key declares `NotSentTrust.KeyReplay` with `keyRetention`, at most that system's documented minimum; the store records the attempts' start on its own clock (the database's, on PostgreSQL). | P3 | Declared a `Lookup` when it is a replay, or a retention longer than the real one, a `notSent()` after the key expired is a new request: a second charge if the first was processed. |
| **A17** | **Redis: one durable primary, no automatic failover** (`@agboss/redis`, #693). The server keeps every acknowledged write (`appendonly yes`, `appendfsync always`, `no-appendfsync-on-rewrite no`), never evicts a key (`maxmemory-policy noeviction`), is a primary and not a cluster — all checked at start — and no Sentinel or operator promotes a replica while instances run. | P1, P4 on Redis leases | A promoted asynchronous replica, an evicted key or a crash without the fsync may lack an epoch the primary acknowledged and grant it again: two holders with one token, a finished lease taken again. `WAIT` does not close it. The opt-out `acknowledged-writes-may-be-lost` says so; [Running-on-Redis](Running-on-Redis), its crash test shows the epoch granted twice. Redis leases never back a fenced write of another backend (A2 refuses it at assembly). |

| **A18** | **The cancel's mark lives in the lease store's database, and the fencing stores read it there** (#909). `ExecutionLeaseRepository.requestCancel` writes it on the backend of the fenced-effect and outbound-call stores (A2), and each of them reads it in the transaction that checks the lease (PostgreSQL: a pair of advisory locks of the tree, the cancel waiting for the tree's open fencing transactions; MongoDB: a write to a per-tree document, so a racing cancel conflicts and the transaction runs again; SQLite: one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`). The fencing stores' retention lifts it only once its tree is over — its lease retired (#520) — and it is older than the cutoff, never by age alone: a tree not over (paused for longer than the retention, resumed on another instance) stays fenced; the mark of a tree never retired, or with no lease row, is kept. | P7 | A store whose fenced write does not read the mark in its lease transaction lets a write through after the cancel answered (`no-effect-after-cancel`, `sabotage-cancel.test.ts`). A cancel written to another database than the fencing stores' fences nothing: `app.cancel` resolves and the holder is stopped only at its next renewal, as before #909. Redis keeps no fencing stores (A2 refuses a fenced write of another backend over Redis leases): its `requestCancel` writes nothing. An instance of an earlier version neither writes nor reads the mark: migrate first (PostgreSQL `0032`, MongoDB schema 4), then roll out. |
| **A19** | **Store commands time out below the framework's bound, and SQLite is on a local disk** (#928). The packages' command timeout (default 10 s, 100–30 000 ms, never off) fires before the framework's 15 s, and PostgreSQL ends a session idle in a transaction after 10 s. | P8 (the bound's cleanliness, not its existence) | The framework still ends the call, but the driver's call stays open; on PostgreSQL a frozen holder's lease row stays locked until the server ends the session, so a takeover or a cancel mark waits for it (liveness). A hung network filesystem under SQLite stalls the process until its probe restarts it. |

## Holds through

- **Clock offsets of any size.** The store decides expiry by its own time, and the holder's fence
  counts elapsed time from when it *sent* the request — the argument holds for any offset; the
  tests sample ± lease/2, ± lease and ± 10 × lease.
- **A late answer.** The fence counts from the request, so a delayed answer shortens what the
  holder trusts, never lengthens it.
- **A crash, SIGKILL, SIGSTOP, a cut database connection** at the fault suite's hold points: its 25
  scenarios stop a pod at fixed points of its store calls, not at every step.
- **A cancel racing an open write** (#909): a write whose transaction was open when the cancel
  was asked either lands first — the cancel answers after it — or is refused at its commit; never
  the write landing after the cancel answered. Both orders are forced by the explorer's cancel
  races, and a held write by the contract case `a cancel asked while the write's transaction is open`.
- **A store that never answers** (#928): a fenced write, an outbound mark, a claim or a lease
  call held for ever — by a test's deaf hold, a stalled database or a black-holed network — ends
  at its bound; under a lease the holder hands over without releasing, and the next holder
  replays or reconciles. Scenarios 22a–22g stop a pod's store at those calls.
- **Any order of the store operations** of two instances, up to the explorer's depth (8 by
  default; 4 on PostgreSQL), and seeded walks of three; both orders of each two-outcome race the
  explorer lists (`races.ts`, `store-races.ts`). Deeper schedules are sampled, not explored.

## What no checker reaches

- **A hung local filesystem under SQLite** (#928): a synchronous call that never returns stalls
  the process — no timer can run — until its probe restarts it (A19).
- **Budgets are not P1–P5.** A budget stops new paid steps; it does not reserve money, so it is
  exceeded by the calls in flight when it ran out, by everything the steps it never holds back
  spend (undos, the limit's own side path) and, across instances, by the steps — of other trees and
  of its own tree on another instance alike — that read the counters before the spend was in the
  ledger, one write after it was made (#727, #732; [FinOps → Nothing is reserved](FinOps)). Like
  A10's slots, an overrun never double-charges.

- **Atomicity inside one store operation** (a check, a round trip, then a write) is outside the
  explorer: it never runs two operations at once. The contracts' race cases and the fault suite
  cover it.
- **The provider's own behaviour** beyond `paymentGatewayChecklist` (G1–G5) is the provider's: run
  the checklist against its sandbox.
- **A6's window.** A send hung past `attemptTimeout` plus the lease can still coincide with a
  resend. Only G1 closes it, and the explorer stops such a send at its sender's fence on purpose,
  so that it checks the stores and not the provider.
